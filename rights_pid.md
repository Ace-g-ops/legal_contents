### Right to interpreter

**Wetin hin mean:**
He mean say you get the right to understand wetin dey happen to you for police station and for court. And, say if you no talk or understand English well, the police or the court must to provide pesin to explain the proceedings for language wey you truly understand. Under Nigerian law, a statement wey if you give out without undersatnding the language, he fit get taken ot thrown out in court. 

**When them dey apply am:**
- For police station, before and during questioning.
- When them read the charge wey dey against you for the first time. 
- For every time wey you appear for court where evidence, rulings, or questions dey presented.
- Before them go ask you to sign or thumbprint any statement

**Wetin you feel say/do:** 
- "Ion no understand English well. I need interpreter."
- "Shey pesin fit explain this thing for language wey i understand ?
- "I no wan sign this statemnet until them read am for language wey i understand."

**Common Wahala:**
- Police fit start proceedings without first checking whether the suspect understand English
- Court sessions fit continue for English even when the accused no understand English
- Pesin fit nod or say "yes" to avoid looking foolish, even when they no understand am.

**Legal basis:**
- - Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999 (as amended), Section 36(6)(a) and (e) — right to be informed promptly in a language understood, and to have the free assistance of an interpreter
- Administration of Criminal Justice Act (ACJA) 2015, Section 6 — notification of cause of arrest and rights of suspect
- Source: https://nigerian-constitution.com/chapter-4/ | https://www.policinglaw.info/assets/downloads/2015_Administration_of_Criminal_Justice_Act.pdf 

### Right to Lawyer

**Wetin e mean:** 
You get the right to follow lawyer talk and to get one present with you - whether or not you fit afford one,you dey entitled to seek legal counsel an if you no fit afford lawyer, you fit request for free legal aid from teh Legal Aid Council or legal NGO. Any statement wey them take from you wey lawyer no dey there(if you ask for one) fit dey challenged for court. 

**When e go dey applied:**
- Immediately  after them arrest pesin, before them go begin question am. 
- When pesin dey make statement for police
- For pesin first court appearance(arraignment)
- Throughout trial and any appeal wey pesin go face

**Wetin pesin fit say/do:**
- "I wan dey silent and I go like to follow any lawyer talk."
- "I wan make lawyer dey present before i answer any questions."
- "I no fit afford any lawyer. I wan make una contact the Legal Aid Council for me."
- "I no go sign anything until my lawyer don see am."

**Common Problems Wey Pesin Fit Face:**
- Police fit pressure suspect say make him/her talk before lawyer go arrive, or them fit discourage them from asking for Lawyer
- Them fit tell suspect say lawyer no dey needed for "small matter" or "just questioning"
- People no really know say legal aid exists and them always dey assume no lawyer mean say them no get rights
- Atimes, statements fit dey taken and signed before lawyer wey them request for arrive

**Legal basis:**
- Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999 (as amended), Section 36(6)(c) — right to defend oneself in person or by a legal practitioner of one's choice
- Administration of Criminal Justice Act (ACJA) 2015 and state Administration of Criminal Justice Laws — right to make a statement to police in the presence of a lawyer of choice
- Legal Aid Act 2011 — establishes the Legal Aid Council for those who cannot afford counsel
- Source: https://nigerian-constitution.com/chapter-4/ | https://www.policinglaw.info/assets/downloads/2015_Administration_of_Criminal_Justice_Act.pdf


### Right to Silence

**Wetin e mean:**
You no dey required to answer questions from police or say anything wey fit incriminate you. You go gast wait until you don follow lawyer talk before you answer any question. If you go talk something, and wetin you talk, them collect am through pressure, threats or oppressive treatment, it fit dey challenged and them fit commot am from your trial completely. 

**When e go dey applied:**
- The moment wey them go arres pesin or detain am even before hem begin question am
- During police interrogation
- When them dey wrie or record any statement
- For trial - them no fit force you to give or produce evidence against yourself

**Wetin you feel do/say:**
- "I go like to dey silent until I don follow my lawyer talk."
- "I no really need to answer that."
- "I dey willing to give una my name and address, but nothing more without my lawyer present."
- If them collect statemnet from you under pressure: "That statement wey them collect no really come from me but was forced and I wan challenge am for court."

**Common Problems:**
- Officers fit dey question suspect after them don them say them no wan talk or them need lawyer. 
- Them fit no tell suspect say them get right to silence before them go begin question am
- Them fit obtain statements through threats, exhaustion, or promises, then present am for court as if say them collect am freely. 

**Legal basis:**
- Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999 (as amended), Section 35(2) — right to remain silent or avoid answering questions until consultation with a legal practitioner
- Constitution, Section 36(11) — no person on trial for a criminal offence can be compelled to give evidence at their own trial
- Evidence Act 2011, Section 28 — defines what counts as a confession
- Evidence Act 2011, Section 29(2)-(3) — a confession is inadmissible if obtained by oppression or by anything likely to make it unreliable, unless the prosecution proves otherwise beyond reasonable doubt; the court can require this proof on its own initiative
- Evidence Act 2011, Section 31 — a confession isn't automatically excluded just because it followed a promise of secrecy, deception, intoxication, or a missed formal caution — relevance still applies (this limits how far the right protects you, so worth knowing)
- Source: https://cjad.nottingham.ac.uk/en/state/100/keyword/310 | https://blog.lawpavilion.com/the-new-tests-of-admissibility-of-confession-under-the-evidence-act-2011 | https://omaplex.com.ng/an-examination-of-the-nigerian-laws-on-confession

**Note:** The 2011 Evidence Act replaced the older "voluntariness" test for confessions with an oppression/unreliability test — legally more precise, but practitioners and even judges still often say "voluntary confession" out of habit. Don't build your content around the word "voluntary" as if it's still the legal standard.


###  Right to Bail

**What it means:**
Being arrested does not mean you must stay in custody until trial. Depending on the offence, you can be released — either by the police (police bail) or by a court (court bail) — often on conditions like a surety, an address, or a bond. Bail conditions are not supposed to be so harsh that they amount to keeping you locked up anyway. Bail is not a punishment and is not supposed to be sold — asking suspects or their families to pay police officers directly for bail is illegal ("bail is free").

**When it applies:**
- After arrest, before you're taken to court, for less serious offences (police bail)
- At your first court appearance, for most offences (court bail)
- While your case is ongoing, if circumstances change (bail pending trial or appeal)
- Note: some serious offences (e.g., capital offences like murder or treason) have bail restricted or made harder to obtain by law

**What you can say/do:**
- "Am I entitled to bail for this offence? On what conditions?"
- "This bail condition is impossible for me to meet — can it be reviewed?"
- "I was told to pay money for bail. I want to know if this is a bribe."
- "How long can I legally be held before I'm either charged, granted bail, or brought to court?"

**Common problems:**
- Police or court officials demand money directly for "bail" (this is illegal — bail itself is free; only bond/surety paperwork may apply)
- Suspects are held well beyond the legal time limit without being charged or brought before a court
- Bail conditions (e.g., requiring a landed-property surety) are set so high that poorer suspects can't meet them and stay in custody anyway
- Suspects don't know they can ask a court to review or vary bail conditions

**Legal basis:**
- Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999 (as amended), Section 35 — right to personal liberty and limits on detention; Section 36(5) — presumption of innocence, which underlies the general right to bail before conviction
- Administration of Criminal Justice Act (ACJA) 2015, Part 2 ("Arrest, Bail and Preventive Justice") — governs bail eligibility, conditions, and timelines for release or arraignment
- Source: https://nigerian-constitution.com/chapter-4/ | https://www.policinglaw.info/assets/downloads/2015_Administration_of_Criminal_Justice_Act.pdf

**Note:** ACJA 2015 is a federal law (applies to FCT and federal offences). States have their own Administration of Criminal Justice Laws (Lagos, Kaduna, Rivers, etc.) which may set different bail conditions — check the specific state law where the arrest happens.

### Right to be informed of Charges

**Wetin e mean:** 
If them arrest you, them go tell you promptly - for any language wey you understand - exactly wetin you dey accused of and why them hold you. E no really optional or something wey police fit delay indefinitely; it must happen for or near the point wey them arrest pesin, not hours or days later. 

**When e dey applied:**
- At the moment wey them arrest pesin
- Before or during any questioning
- For pesin first appearance for court, when them go read the formal charge.

**Wetin pesin fit say or do:**
- "Wetin them arrest for exactly ? I wan know, abegg tell me wetin I really do."
- "I wan make them explain this thing for language wey I understand before I go answer any question."
- "Shey i fit see the charge in writing?"

**Common Problems:**
- Them dey arrest people and also hold them without tellling them wetin them arrest them for("Come to the station, we'll explain there")
- Them dey atimes give unclear accusations ("you know what you did").
- Them go question suspects for hours before them mention any charges
- Them dey change charges later without the suspect being clearly informed of the change

**Legal basis:**
- Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999 (as amended), Section 35(3) — persons arrested must be informed in writing of the facts and grounds for arrest within 24 hours; Section 36(6)(a) — right to be informed promptly of the details of the offence charged
- Administration of Criminal Justice Act (ACJA) 2015, Section 6 — notification of cause of arrest and rights of suspect
- Source: https://nigerian-constitution.com/chapter-4/ | https://www.policinglaw.info/assets/downloads/2015_Administration_of_Criminal_Justice_Act.pdf   

## Right to Fair Hearing

**Wetin e mean:** 
Any pesin wey dey face court case - civil or criminal dey entitled to get their case heard within a reasonable time by a court or tribunal wey dey genuinely independent and impartial, and to get the proceedings done for public. Thi na the umbrella right wey everything else in a trila(interpreter, lawyer, being informed of the charge) dey under, but it still dey on hin own: a trial fit dey technically "fair" in the sense wey be say the following procedures fir dey breached if them drag am for years, or if the judge tribunal get stake for the outcome.

**When e dey applied:**
- For the entire time of a criminal or civil case, wey begin from the first appearance to final judgement
- When court or tribunal make decision wey go affect pesin rights or obligations
- For the venue and manner of the hearing itself - proceedings and the announcement of decisions must happen for public, and not in private chambers
- To the composition of the court/tribunal - it must dey set up in a way wey be say e go secure independence and impartiality

**Wetin pesin fit say/do:**
- "This case don dey delayed for years without progress- that na breach of my right to a hearing without reasonable time."
- "I no believe this tribunal go fit dey impartial, given [specific conflict of interest]. I wan make them raise am for record." 
- "This proceedings must dey held for public. Why them con dey conduct am privately."
- "The judgement/decisons must dey delivered for open court, not for chambers."

**Common Problems:**
- Cases wey dey stall in awaiting for years, especially where the acccused dey for custody, defeating the "reasonable time" requirement
- A judge, tribunal member, or panel fit get personal or institutional stake for the outcome, wey go undermine impartiality.
- Proceedings or judgments dey delivered for judge's chambers rather than for open court, which then mean say e no fit satisfy "public" hearing
- Virtual/remote court sittings dey raise unresolved questions about wetin counts as "public" under this section

**Legal basis:**
- Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999 (as amended), Section 36(1) — right to fair hearing within a reasonable time, by a court/tribunal constituted to secure independence and impartiality
- Section 36(3) — proceedings and the announcement of decisions must be held in public
- Section 36(4) — anyone charged with a criminal offence is entitled to a fair hearing in public within a reasonable time, subject to narrow exceptions (defence, public safety/order/morality, protecting minors or private lives, or where publicity would harm the interests of justice)
- Related but separately covered elsewhere: Section 36(6) (informed of charge, lawyer, interpreter — see their own files); Section 36(11) (no compulsion to give evidence at own trial — see right-to-silence file)
- Source: https://nigerian-constitution.com/?p=512 | https://legalpediaonline.com/?p=3308 (Fabian Imoh v. The State, on "chambers" not satisfying "public")

### Right to Humane Treatment / Dignity of Person

**Wetin e mean:** 
Every pesin dey entitled to get their dignity respected, no matter wetun them accuse them of. This means say, no torture, and no cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment - at the point of arrest, during questioning, or while in custody. This right dey applied to unconditionally; e no be something wey go dey suspended because say pesin na suspect for serious crime

**When e de applied:**
- During the arrest itself - no excessive force dey permitted
- During police questioning or interrogation
- While pesin dey for custody, including conditions of detention(food, access to mediical care, physical treatment)
- At any point during the criminal justice process, including trial and post-conviction
