
## Document: Charge Sheet

**What this document is:**
A charge sheet is the formal document that lists the crime(s) you are accused of. It is filed by the prosecution and read in court at your first appearance. You have the right to receive a copy within 7 days of filing.

**Legal basis:**
Administration of Criminal Justice Act (ACJA) 2015, Sections 193-196

**When you'll see it:**
- At your first court appearance
- When charges are being filed against you
- Any time charges are amended or upgrade
  
**Key sections and what they mean:**

| Section | What it means | What to check |
|---------|---------------|---------------|
| **Heading** | Name of the court (e.g., "IN THE MAGISTRATE COURT OF LAGOS") | Is this the correct court for your case? |
| **Charge Number** | Reference number for the case (e.g., "CHARGE NO: ABC/123/2024") | Write this down — you'll need it for all future court visits |
| **Parties** | Who is prosecuting you (e.g., "The People of Lagos State") and your name | Is your name spelled correctly? |
| **Statement of Offence** | Name of the crime (e.g., "Stealing", "Assault", "Criminal Damage") | Does this match what police told you? |
| **Particulars of Offence** | Details: when, where, what happened, who was affected | Are the date, place, and details accurate? |
| **Section of Law** | Which law you allegedly broke (e.g., "Section 516 Criminal Code") | Ask your lawyer to explain what this law means |
| **Date** | When the charge was filed | Should be soon after your arrest (usually within days) |
| **Signature** | Signature of prosecutor or police officer who filed the charge | Must be signed — unsigned charges may be invalid |


**How to check if it's correct:**

- [ ] Is your full name spelled correctly?
- [ ] Is the date of the alleged offence accurate?
- [ ] Is the place/location correct?
- [ ] Does the charge match what police told you at arrest?
- [ ] Is the court name correct (should be in the area where offence happened)?
- [ ] Is it signed by a prosecutor or police officer?
- [ ] Did you receive a copy? (You're entitled to one)

**What to do if something is wrong:**

1. **Tell your lawyer immediately** — They can object to defective charges
2. **Ask the court to note the error** — The judge should be aware
3. **Don't sign anything** until errors are corrected
4. **Request a corrected copy** — You're entitled to accurate charge documents
5. **If charge is completely different** - if the charges is completly differnt from what you were arrested for, ask your lawyer to challenge it

**Red flags (suspicious changes to watch for):**

 **High priority (could indicate tampering):**
- Date of alleged offence changed without explanation
- Charge upgraded to a more serious offence suddenly
- Your name or identity details altered
- Court name changed (could mean case was moved without your knowledge)
- Missing signature or court stamp
- No charge number or reference

 **Medium priority:**
- Spelling mistakes in your name or address
- Wrong date format
- Minor formatting changes

**If you spot suspicious changes:**
1. Compare with any earlier copies you have
2. Ask your lawyer to verify with the court registry
3. Request certified true copies from the court
4. Don't plead until the charge is clarified

**Your rights regarding charge sheets:**

- Right to receive a copy of the charge (ACJA 2015)
- Right to understand the charge (in a language you understand)
- Right to challenge defective or unclear charges
- Right to have the charge read to you in court  

**Sample Sheet**: 


IN THE MAGISTRATE COURT OF LAGOS
CHARGE NO: ABC/123/2024

THE PEOPLE OF LAGOS STATE vs. JOHN

STATEMENT OF OFFENCE:
Stealing

PARTICULARS OF OFFENCE:
That you, John Doe, on the 15th day of January 2024, at Lagos Island, stole a mobile phone valued at ₦50,000 belonging to Mary Smith, thereby committing an offence punishable under Section 516 of the Criminal Code.

Dated this 20th day of January 2024

[Signature]

Public Prosecutor


**Sources:**
- Administration of Criminal Justice Act (ACJA) 2015, Sections 193-196
- 1999 Constitution of Nigeria, Section 36 (Right to Fair Hearing)
- FCT High Court Practice Direction on ACJA 2015


## Document: Bail Order

**What this document is:**

A bail order is a court document that sets the conditions under which you can be released from custody while waiting for your trial. It specifies how much money or property you (or your sureties) must pledge, and what rules you must follow while on bail.

**Legal basis:**
Administration of Criminal Justice Act (ACJA) 2015, Part 19 (Sections 158-188)


**When you'll see it:**
- After your first appearance when the judge considers bail
- When bail is granted by the court
- When the bail conditions are varied or are updated
- When sureties are being approved

**Key Sections and they mean:**

| Section | What it means | What to check |
|---------|---------------|---------------|
| **Heading** | Name of the court (e.g., "IN THE HIGH COURT OF LAGOS STATE") | Is this the correct court handling your case? |
| **Case Number** | Reference number for your case (e.g., "CR/123/2024") | Should match the charge sheet number |
| **Defendant's Name** | Your full name | Is it spelled correctly? |
| **Date of Order** | When the bail order was made | Should be on or after your first appearance |
| **Bail Amount** | How much money/property is required (e.g., "₦500,000 with two sureties") | Is this what the judge announced in court? |
| **Number of Sureties** | How many people must stand as guarantors for you (e.g., "two sureties in the sum of ₦250,000 each") | Does this match what was ordered? |
| **Conditions of Bail** | Rules you must follow while on bail (e.g., "surrender passport", "report to police station weekly") | Read carefully — breaking these can send you back to custody |
| **Sureties' Requirements** | What your sureties must provide (e.g., valid ID, proof of address, oath) | Make sure your sureties understand what's required |
| **Signature** | Signature of the judge or magistrate who made the order | Must be signed and dated |
| **Court Stamp** | Official stamp of the court | Should have the court's seal/stamp |

**Common bail conditions (what you might see):**

- Surrender your passport (if you have one)
- Report to a designated police station at specified times (e.g., weekly or monthly)
- Not leave the city/state without court permission
- Provide one or more sureties (guarantors) who will sign the bail bond
- Pay a specified amount of money or deposit property
- Not contact witnesses or interfere with the case
- Reside at a specific address while on bail

**How to check if it's correct:**

- [ ] Is your full name spelled correctly?
- [ ] Does the case number match your charge sheet?
- [ ] Is the bail amount what the judge announced?
- [ ] Are the conditions reasonable and what was discussed in court?
- [ ] Is the number of sureties correct?
- [ ] Is it signed by the judge/magistrate?
- [ ] Does it have the court stamp/seal?
- [ ] Is the date accurate?

**What to do if something is wrong:**

1. **Tell your lawyer immediately** — They can apply to vary (change) the bail order
2. **Don't sign the bail bond** - don' until errors are corrected
3. **Ask the court to correct the order** — Minor errors (typos) can be fixed
4. **If bail amount is different** - if the bail amount is differnet from what judge announced, your lawyer should raise it before you leave court
5. **Request a certified true copy** — Always get an official copy from the court registry


**Red Flags(Suspicious changes to watch for):**

**High Priorites(Could indicate tamepering):**
- Bail amount changed without your knowledge or court hearing
- Conditions added that the judge didn't mention
- Your name or case number altered
- Missing judge's signature or court stamp
- Date doesn't match when you were actually granted bail
- Sureties' requirements changed (e.g., from 2 sureties to 4)

**Medium Priorities:**
- Spelling mistakes in your name or address
- Wrong date format
- Typographical errors in amounts (e.g., ₦50,000 vs ₦500,000)

**If you spot suspicious changes:**
1. Compare with any notes you took in court when bail was granted
2. Ask your lawyer to verify with the court registry
3. Request a certified true copy from the court
4. Don't pay any money or bring sureties until the order is verified

**Your rights regarding bail:**
- Right to be considered for bail (ACJA 2015, Section 158)
- Right to know the bail conditions clearly
- Right to apply for variation of bail if conditions are too harsh
- Right to have bail reconsidered if your circumstances change
- Right to be released once bail conditions are met (ACJA 2015, Section 171)

**Important notes about bail:**

**Bail is NOT automatic for all offences:**
- **Capital offences** (e.g., murder, armed robbery): Bail is at the judge's discretion (ACJA S.161)
- **Offences punishable by 3+ years**: Bail may be granted based on circumstances (ACJA S.162)
- **Offences punishable by less than 3 years**: You're generally entitled to bail (ACJA S.163)

**What happens if you break bail conditions:**
- Your bail can be revoked
- You can be arrested and returned to custody
- Your sureties may lose their money/property
- You may face additional charges

**What happens to bail money:**
- If you attend all court dates: Bail is discharged, sureties are released
- If you skip court: Bail money/property may be forfeited to the court

**Sample Bail Order format:**
IN THE HIGH COURT OF LAGOS STATE
HOLDEN AT IKEJA

CR/123/2024

THE PEOPLE OF LAGOS STATE vs. JOHN DOE

ORDER FOR BAIL

Upon the application of the defendant for bail, and upon the defendant entering into a recognizance in the sum of ₦500,000 with two sureties in the sum of ₦250,000 each, it is ordered as follows:

1. The defendant is hereby granted bail in the sum of ₦500,000 with two (2) sureties.

2. Each surety must be a person of good character and provide:

   - Valid means of identification

   - Proof of address

   - Sworn affidavit of means

3. Conditions of bail:

   - The defendant shall surrender his passport to the court

   - The defendant shall report weekly to the nearest police station

   - The defendant shall not leave Lagos State without permission of this court

   - The defendant shall attend all court hearings

4. This bail is granted pursuant to Section 162 of the Administration of Criminal Justice Act, 2015.

Dated this 25th day of March 2024

Hon. [ ]

High Court of Lagos State

[Court Stamp]

**Sources:**
- Administration of Criminal Justice Act (ACJA) 2015, Part 19 (Sections 158-188)

  - Section 161: Bail for capital offences
  - Section 162: Bail for offences exceeding 3 years imprisonment
  - Section 163: Bail for offences not exceeding 3 years imprisonment
  - Section 165: Conditions for bail
  - Section 171: Release on execution of recognizance
  - Section 179: Forfeiture of recognizance
- 1999 Constitution of Nigeria, Section 35 (Right to personal liberty)
- FCT High Court Practice Direction on ACJA 2015
